![Website Screenshot](/img/rempelsts.jpg) I have been using Linux for quite a
few years now. I figure it was sometime around 2003 that I began using Mepis at
the behest of a fellow nerd I met one winter at snow camp. As I recall, one of
the biggest challenges was finding software to replace the things I was used to
using.

It didn't take long before I had a list of my favorite open source software, at
which point returning to Windows could be somewhat annoying as some of that
software didn't exist for Windows. Today, my list of favorite software is
somewhat different than it was when I was in high school, and as I can be
rather forgetful sometimes, I thought it might be helpful to create a list for
future reference.

### Simple Scan
![Simple Scan Screenshot](/img/simpleScan.jpg)
For scanning printed documents and converting them to PDF, I am not sure that
Simple Scan (pacman -S simple-scan) can be beat. You click scan and BOOM, there
is the sheet you were scanning. You can crop, reorient it, and then if there is
another page, simply click "scan" again and "BOOM", now you have another page.
Are the pages in the wrong order? No problem! Click and drag and now your pages
are in the RIGHT order.

In the bad old days, I used to use LibreOffice Draw to convert my documents,
but that involves so many more steps per page... it is actually quite tedious
and time consuming compared to simple scan. And as I am someone who cannot keep
physical paper organized (and it just piles up and takes up so much space), I
love being able to quickly and easily digitize my documents before shredding
them. Now, if only I can convince people to send me documents digitally in the
first place...

### VIM
I used to use nano exclusively. But after watching videos by
[Luke Smith](www.lukesmith.xyz), I was convinced to give VIM an earnest try. So
far, I am growing to like it. I tried using nano this afternoon, but found that
as my muscle memory is starting to adapt to giving commands in VIM, nano was
actually more difficult for me to use. I have a long way to go in developing my
overall proficiency in VIM, but I can certainly see why so many people prefer
it.

### KeePassX
Years ago I read a Maximum PC article about password managers. The approach
they recommended was to use KeePass with one password and DropBox with another
such that you would always have access to your password database even if your
computer unexpectedly dies. While I no longer use dropbox, this is the same
basic approach I have taken.

I did discover that - in Parabola - one must install the keepassxc or keepassx2
package to avoid using the old 0.4.4-3 version.

### HUGO
Inspired by [Bryan Lunduke](www.lunduke.com), I decided to play with the idea
of pre-generated HTML only webpages. After some work, I was able to get this
web-page imported into HUGO, and I now use that and plain-text markdown files
to manage this webpage.
